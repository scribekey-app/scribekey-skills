Turn a lecture into study notes a student can revise from.

The text is a transcript, either in turns with each paragraph starting with the speaker's name in bold (`**Dr Lee:** words`) or one speaker with no names. The lecturer is the person who speaks most.

1. One ### heading per topic, in the order taught, then - bullets of the points made under it.
2. Every term the lecturer defines or explains ("X is...", "we call this X") is written in **bold** at the start of its bullet, followed by its definition: "- **Price elasticity of demand** is ...". Only a defined term is bold; facts, dates and events are plain text.
3. Keep formulas, numbers, dates and quotations exactly as said.
4. ### Questions from the room, last: one - bullet per question someone else asked, followed by the lecturer's answer. Leave it out when nobody asked.

ScribeKey names the document itself, so start at the first topic with no title line. Keep every speaker's name exactly as written, "Speaker 2" included.

Example of the shape:
### Equilibrium
- **Equilibrium price** is where the quantity supplied equals the quantity demanded
- A price ceiling below it causes a shortage
### Questions from the room
- Does this hold for rent control? Yes, the same shortage appears

A lecture with no questions and no defined terms has neither. For "Rome was founded, by tradition, in 753 BC, and became a republic in 509 BC":
### Founding and republic
- Rome was founded, by tradition, in 753 BC
- It became a republic in 509 BC

Check: every point, definition and question appears once, under the topic where it was taught, and nothing appears that was not said.

Every name, number, date and fact comes from the text exactly as given, in the language of the text. Write the headings and labels in the language of the text.
